# Community

Joshternet started with the rather entertaining idea of Joshes finding other
Joshes across the independent web. Along the way, it became something bigger: a
way to meet interesting people, discover what they're making, and connect
independent websites without asking anyone to give up their own corner of the
internet.

That only works if the people involved treat one another with a little respect.
Joshternet welcomes Joshes, non-Joshes, unusual ideas, disagreement,
experimentation, and the occasional terrible joke. Everyone is welcome to join
in the joshing. What it doesn't welcome is using those freedoms to threaten,
exploit, or deliberately harm other people.

These principles explain the kind of community Joshternet is trying to build and
the responsibilities that come with maintaining its shared spaces and services.
Independent websites remain independent, and participation does not transfer
ownership or control to the project.

This page is the living community commitment for Joshternet-operated spaces and
shared infrastructure. It is not a protocol specification. It does not replace
the RFCs, define who is a Josh, or give Joshternet authority over independently
operated websites. A community conduct rule is not automatically a technical
requirement for publishing `/.well-known/josh`. The full rules below stay
complete on purpose—protections are not cut for length.

## Community principles

### People come before the network

The interesting part of Joshternet isn't the crawler, the registry, or the
specifications. It's the people behind the websites and the things they create.
All the technical work should help people find one another, share ideas, and
discover more of the independent web.

Specifications, crawlers, registries, directories, websites, and other
infrastructure are tools toward that purpose. They should never become more
important than the people they are intended to serve. Technology should
contribute to human creativity, knowledge, connection, and opportunity.

### Participation is voluntary

Nobody should have to ask permission to join, and nobody should have to justify
leaving. Your website, content, infrastructure, and identity remain yours.

People choose whether and how they participate. No one should be pressured to
remain listed, publish additional information, or interact with another
participant.

Withdrawing a declaration should be respected by Joshternet-operated discovery
services as those systems update. That does not promise instantaneous removal
from every independent registry, cache, or copy elsewhere on the internet, and
there is no central membership-cancellation account to close.

### Identity belongs to the person

Joshness is declared, never derived.

No maintainer, crawler, registry, algorithm, contributor, or other Josh gets to
decide someone else's Josh identity. A person's name, domain, biography, or
popularity does not determine whether they are a Josh.

Affirmed, Declined, and Undeclared Josh identity remain separate from
participation. Non-Joshes can participate without being represented as Joshes.
People should be free to change or withdraw their declaration without having to
justify that decision to the community.

For the technical identity model, see [RFC-JOSH-0001][rfc-identity].

### No Josh outranks another Josh

The Joshternet does not exist to establish a hierarchy of Joshes.

Wealth, popularity, traffic, technical ability, tenure, contribution history, or
proximity to project maintainers does not make one Josh more Josh than another.

Contributing more may create additional operational responsibility—repository
access, infrastructure work, or shared services. That access exists to do the
work. It does not create additional Joshness.

Friends of Joshes—Non-Joshes who join in the joshing—stand as equals. Declining
Josh identity does not lower anyone's place in the Network.

### Independent places matter

The most interesting parts of Joshternet are the places people have made for
themselves. Personal websites, photographs, writing, software, strange little
experiments, and things that don't fit neatly into a category are what make the
journey worthwhile.

Joshternet should help visitors find those places, not try to pull everything
into one more centralized service. The project does not own or republish
participant work as a substitute for the original sites.

## Freedom of expression and responsibility

### Expression should be broad

Joshternet values a web where people can express ideas, opinions, beliefs,
criticism, humor, art, disagreement, and unpopular points of view.

People should be able to question specifications, institutions, cultural
practices, political or philosophical ideas, technologies, community decisions,
and one another's arguments without fearing removal merely because the discussion
is uncomfortable or unpopular. Maintainers are not exempt from criticism.

Being offended by an idea is not, by itself, evidence that community rules have
been violated. Disagreement is not harassment. Criticism is not abuse.

People will disagree about specifications, implementations, governance, design,
moderation, culture, and probably the correct pluralization of Josh. Being wrong
should be survivable. Changing your mind should be encouraged. People should be
able to say difficult things respectfully, and other people should be able to
challenge those things just as openly.

### Expression does not excuse harmful conduct

Freedom of expression does not provide permission to threaten people, stalk them,
expose private information, exploit children, organize harassment, defraud
others, or deliberately place people in danger.

The distinction should generally be based on conduct and identifiable harm rather
than whether a viewpoint is popular. Community rules should therefore be applied
to behavior as consistently as practical, regardless of the opinions, beliefs,
identities, politics, or affiliations of the people involved.

### Independent publishing remains independent

Joshternet does not control what participants publish on independently operated
websites. Participation does not make Joshternet the editor, publisher, or owner
of those sites.

At the same time, no Joshternet-operated service is required to host, index,
recommend, display, or distribute every piece of content it encounters. A crawler
declining to index something is not redefining the publisher's identity. A
community space removing material from that space does not remove it from the
web.

<p class="prose-emphasis"><strong>Interoperability does not require interaction, and freedom of expression does not require compelled amplification.</strong></p>

## Respect and acceptable behavior

Participants in Joshternet-operated community spaces should:

- engage honestly and in good faith;
- disagree with ideas without turning disagreements into personal attacks;
- respect another person's privacy and personal boundaries;
- allow people to disengage from conversations;
- welcome questions and newcomers;
- criticize decisions, implementations, specifications, and institutions directly when criticism is warranted;
- make reasonable efforts to understand people from different backgrounds and experiences;
- correct mistakes when they become apparent;
- avoid intentionally disrupting discussions or preventing others from participating;
- consider the audience and context when sharing sensitive material.

Directness is welcome. Disagreement is welcome. Criticism is welcome. Personal
intimidation is not.

## Behavior we will not accept

Joshternet-operated community spaces and services should not be used for:

- credible threats or encouragement of violence against another person;
- stalking, intimidation, or sustained targeted harassment;
- publishing another person's sensitive private information without permission;
- sexual exploitation or abuse of children;
- grooming or sexualization of minors;
- distributing child sexual abuse material;
- non-consensual intimate imagery or sexual exploitation;
- fraud, phishing, impersonation for malicious purposes, or deliberate scams;
- malware or activity intended to compromise another person's systems;
- repeated unwanted sexual attention;
- deliberate attempts to drive someone from the community through coordinated abuse;
- sustained disruption intended to prevent others from participating;
- knowingly using Joshternet infrastructure to facilitate unlawful exploitation or serious harm.

These rules apply to conduct rather than requiring everyone to share the same
beliefs.

A person should not be removed merely for disagreeing with another participant,
challenging prevailing opinions, expressing an unpopular idea, or criticizing the
project or its maintainers.

## Children, vulnerable people, and audience responsibility

The open web reaches people of many ages, backgrounds, cultures, and
circumstances. Joshternet-operated public spaces should therefore be suitable for
the audiences they are intended to serve.

Content involving explicit sexuality, graphic violence, or other material
reasonably unsuitable for general audiences should not be unexpectedly presented
in general community spaces. Where legitimate mature material is relevant,
services should use appropriate context, warnings, labeling, filtering, or age
restrictions where practical.

Material exploiting children has no place in Joshternet's services or community
spaces. Protecting children and people vulnerable to exploitation takes priority
over providing distribution for exploitative material.

Audience awareness should not become an excuse to remove difficult ideas,
educational material, legitimate artistic work, historical information, health
information, or serious discussion simply because those subjects may be
uncomfortable.

Context matters.

## Make room for different ways of participating

You don't have to write code, maintain a server, or even be named Josh to have
something worthwhile to contribute. The independent web is interesting because
people bring different skills, interests, and ideas to it.

People may contribute through websites, testing, documentation, design,
discussion, implementation experience, artwork, events, community work,
criticism, or simply by participating in the network. Participating in the
network by publishing a declaration is not the same as contributing to project
development, and neither path requires the other.

Joshternet should remain understandable and approachable enough that participation
does not require belonging to a particular technical culture.

## Keep the web human

Joshternet can use automation without becoming a network designed for machines.

Crawlers can discover sites. Validators can check declarations. Software can help
maintain infrastructure. The destination should still be people.

And because this is, after all, a network of Joshes built on the web, there
should always be room for experimentation, personality, weird websites, bad
jokes, and having some fun with the whole thing.

## Stewardship commitment

Stewardship is responsibility for the project. It is not ownership of the network.

Anyone entrusted with maintaining Joshternet repositories, specifications,
domains, services, registries, crawlers, community spaces, or other shared
infrastructure should accept the following commitments.

### We will steward the project, not control the network

The Joshternet project may maintain specifications and shared infrastructure.

It does not own participating websites, participant identities, or the network
created when independent sites interoperate.

No project service should become a required gatekeeper for participation.

### We will protect participant independence

Participating websites should remain independently operable.

Shared infrastructure should be replaceable wherever practical. A registry,
crawler, directory, validator, domain, repository, or organization should not
become an authority simply because it is widely used.

The network should be able to outlive any particular implementation.

### We will not turn infrastructure into identity authority

JoshBot may discover a Josh.

A registry may record a Josh.

A validator may confirm that a declaration follows a specification.

None of them create a Josh.

Operational decisions about indexing, security, abuse, moderation, or interaction
must remain separate from a person's authority to declare their own Josh
identity.

### We will protect people from abuse

The commitment to open expression does not require the project to provide
infrastructure for exploitation, threats, harassment, fraud, or serious harm.

When intervention is necessary, stewards should act against the harmful conduct
as narrowly as reasonably possible.

Responses should consider context, severity, repetition, intent where it can
reasonably be established, and actual or reasonably foreseeable harm.

Restrictions should not be broader than necessary merely because broader
restrictions are administratively easier.

### We will protect open expression

Stewards should not use moderation, infrastructure control, repository access, or
administrative authority to silence criticism, settle personal disagreements, or
enforce ideological conformity.

Project decisions may be challenged. Maintainers may be criticized.
Specifications may be questioned. Community members may disagree with one
another.

Stewardship requires tolerating disagreement, including disagreement directed at
the stewards themselves.

### We will apply community rules fairly

Community rules should describe behavior clearly enough that people can
reasonably understand what is expected of them.

Enforcement should not depend on whether a steward agrees with someone's
opinions.

Similar conduct should receive similar consideration.

People accused of ordinary community violations should, where safety permits, be
told what conduct created the problem and given a reasonable opportunity to
respond.

Serious threats, exploitation, illegal material, security emergencies, or
immediate risks of harm may require immediate action.

### We will distinguish stewardship from privilege

Maintainers may need additional permissions to operate repositories,
infrastructure, community spaces, or services.

Those permissions exist to perform work on behalf of the project.

They do not establish greater Joshness, permanent authority, or ownership of the
community.

### We will resist unnecessary centralization

Convenience will sometimes justify shared services.

Convenience alone should not quietly turn those services into mandatory
infrastructure.

When the project introduces centralized components, we should ask whether they
can remain optional, replaceable, independently implementable, or reproducible by
others.

### We will minimize what we collect

Joshternet should collect and retain only information reasonably necessary for
the services it provides.

Participation should not require unnecessary personal information.

Where infrastructure stores public information discovered from participating
sites, its purpose and behavior should be documented clearly. Operational detail
lives on the [Privacy][privacy] and [Security][security] pages.

### We will make important project decisions in public

Specifications, proposals, significant architectural changes, community rules,
and governance changes should be developed through publicly reviewable processes
whenever practical.

Decisions should leave a history that later contributors can understand.

Disagreement should not disappear simply because a maintainer has merge access.

Privacy, security incidents, abuse reports, information concerning minors, and
similarly sensitive matters may require confidential handling.

Transparency does not require exposing victims or private information.

### We will protect the ability to leave

A participant should be able to stop participating without asking permission from
Joshternet.

Removing an authoritative participation declaration should be respected by
Joshternet-operated systems as those systems update.

Leaving the network should not require surrendering a domain, account, identity,
content, or anything else the participant already owned. Withdrawal does not
automatically delete previously published information from the wider internet.

### We will not sell standing in the network

Money, sponsorship, advertising, employment, donations, or commercial
relationships must not determine whether someone is recognized as a Josh, whether
a valid implementation conforms to a specification, or where someone stands
relative to another participant.

Shared infrastructure may eventually require funding.

Funding must not purchase Joshness or superior standing.

### We will plan for stewardship beyond the founder

Joshternet should not depend forever on one person being available.

As sustained independent participation develops, stewardship should expand with
it. Responsibilities, access, decision-making processes, and succession should
become more explicit as the community demonstrates the need for them.

Governance should grow from actual participation rather than inventing
representatives, boards, or voting systems for a community that does not yet
exist.

### We will leave room to improve this commitment

This document is a starting point, not a claim that every future community
question has already been answered.

Changes should be discussed publicly and should preserve the fundamental
relationship between Joshternet and its participants:

The people own their places.

The people own their identities.

People should be free to speak and free to disagree.

Freedom should be exercised with responsibility toward other people.

Children and vulnerable people should be protected from exploitation.

Stewards take care of the project.

No steward owns the network.

No Josh outranks another Josh.

And Joshternet should remain just weird enough to be worth wandering through.
